How Online Games Distinguish Players Within the Same Session

Participant identification inside a shared digital table environment

A shared digital card table contains actions from multiple participants, yet every action has a specific source — a specific player who submitted it. The software cannot treat all incoming actions as coming from a single undifferentiated group. Each action must be attributed to the correct participant so that the game can apply it appropriately: to the right hand, at the right turn, with the right effect on that specific player's position. This attribution depends on the system having a reliable way to identify each individual participant within the shared session.

Why Individual Identification Is Necessary Inside a Session

A multiplayer card game at the table level is fundamentally about individual participants taking turns, each acting from their own position with their own cards, contributing to a shared game state that every participant experiences. The game logic needs to know whose cards are whose, whose turn is current, who submitted each action, and whose interface needs to show which information. All of this depends on individual participant identity being clearly established and maintained throughout the session.

Without per-participant identification, the game logic would have no way to associate cards with their owners, no way to enforce turn order, and no way to validate that an action is coming from the player whose turn it currently is. The shared session would collapse into an undifferentiated pool of actions with no coherent attribution.

Two Layers of Identity: Account and Session

Player identity in an online card game operates at two distinct levels. The first is account-level identity — the persistent identity that a player has across all their sessions on the platform. An account identity persists between sessions, carries historical records, and represents the player as a continuing participant in the platform's ecosystem.

The second is session-level identity — the temporary identity a player holds within one specific table session. When a player joins a table, the system creates a session-level identity for them within that specific table. This session identity connects their account to the specific seat position they occupy in the session, tracks their participation for the duration of the session, and expires when the session ends.

Account-Level Identity

Persistent across all sessions. Carries historical records, preferences, and platform-level information. Identifies the player as a platform participant regardless of which table they are at.

Session-Level Identity

Temporary — exists only for the duration of one table session. Links account identity to a specific seat position within a specific table. Expires when the session ends.

Seat Positions and Their Role

Within a table session, participants are typically assigned to seat positions that represent their placement around the virtual table. A seat position is distinct from account identity — it represents where the player sits within the spatial and logical structure of the game. Seat positions determine dealing order, turn sequence, and the spatial arrangement of the table view from each player's perspective.

The combination of session identifier and seat position provides the most precise identification of a participant within a specific game context. "Table T001, Seat 3" unambiguously identifies one specific player in one specific session — more precisely than either the table identifier alone or the player's account identity alone.

Why Each Participant's Actions Are Kept Separate

The separation of participant activity within a shared session serves multiple purposes simultaneously. It allows turn order enforcement — the system can verify that only the player whose turn it currently is can submit actions for that turn. It allows card assignment — each player's hand is associated with their session identity so that only their interface shows their specific cards face-up. It allows result attribution — when a round concludes, the result can be recorded correctly against each participant's account history.

  • Card assignment — each player's dealt cards are linked to their session identity, ensuring only they see their hand face-up
  • Turn enforcement — the system validates that submitted actions come from the player whose turn is currently active
  • Result attribution — session outcomes are recorded against each participant's account for accurate historical records
  • State delivery — game state updates are delivered correctly to each participant based on their individual session identity and visibility rules
  • Action validation — the system can check whether the submitting participant is eligible for the requested action based on their current session status

Handling Participant Disconnection and Reconnection

Session-level identity also enables reconnection handling. When a participant loses their connection and reconnects, the system can recognize them by their account identity and restore their session-level identity within the active table. Their seat position is retained, their hand remains associated with their session identity, and their interface can be restored to accurately reflect the current game state from their specific participant perspective.

Without stable session-level identity, reconnection would require the player to rejoin as a new participant — potentially from a different seat position, potentially losing their current hand, and definitely disrupting the ongoing round for all participants. Session identity persistence is what makes transparent reconnection — where the player seamlessly re-enters the session from where they left — technically possible.

Key design relationship: Account identity and session identity must both be present for correct game operation. Account identity alone does not specify seat position or turn order within a specific table. Session identity alone does not carry the persistent player information needed for result attribution. The combination of the two, linked at session join time, is what provides complete participant identification throughout a session's lifecycle.

Visibility Rules and Participant Identity

Participant identity is also the mechanism through which card visibility rules are applied correctly. When the server prepares a state update for delivery to a specific participant, it uses that participant's session identity to determine which cards should appear face-up and which should appear face-down in their view. Cards belonging to this participant are shown face-up in their view. Cards belonging to other participants appear face-down unless a game event has made them shared-visible.

The state update delivered to each participant is therefore individualized — it reflects the same shared game state but presents it from that specific participant's informational perspective. This per-participant customization of the state delivery depends entirely on reliable participant identification. Without it, the system would have no basis for determining which cards each interface should show and which it should conceal.
